01Where the Council Agrees

Confirmed dead

The warm-laptop, two-device use case — killed by its own logic, not by your instruction. All five advisors independently rejected it. The Outsider gave the reason the document never addressed: the README says the laptop "stays in the building," and treats that as if it meant within arm's reach. It doesn't. Emergencies happen in bathrooms, bedrooms, stairwells. Nobody stands next to the laptop when someone collapses, and nobody leaves the patient to go find it. Your constraint was correct before you justified it.

Almost none of the emergency-path value requires a model at runtime. The First Principles Thinker counted the bits and the count is not arguable. Address, room, age, sex, conditions: already in profile.json, zero entropy. Consciousness, breathing, circulation: forced buttons, and the model is explicitly banned from touching them. What remains for Whisper plus a 3-billion-parameter model over an 884-token prompt is one selection out of 28 — roughly five bits.

The real deliverable is already built, and it is 4 MB. 49 pre-rendered WAVs (verified: 44 in data/audio/, 5 in data/audio/profile/) plus a 31-term human-verified ontology. Your README already concedes the app "never synthesises speech at runtime." The classifier does not produce Japanese — it picks which file to play.

C8 is not a wall, it is a booking form. our ward and Edogawa fire stations run free, publicly-enrollable 普通救命講習 — three hours in a room with a serving firefighter. You rated expert access 99.9% infeasible. You were wrong, and this is the cheapest correction available to you this week.

A full re-platform in seven days is impossible. Porting Whisper + Qwen2.5-3B to a phone is weeks of llama.cpp/ONNX work and destroys OpenVINO (prefix caching is x64-only). You do not need to: the WAVs are already rendered, and a tap-driven PWA over a closed audio set is HTML and <audio>.

02Where the Council Clashes

Clash 1 — Does a laminated card make the product unnecessary?

The Contrarian's sharpest attack: the only gap you claim over 三者間同時通訳 is the room number and the medical history, and a laminated fridge card delivers that at 0% of the technology. The First Principles Thinker refuted it in one line, and the dissent reviewer confirmed it:

A caller who cannot read Japanese cannot pronounce 〇〇区〇〇.

Romaji read aloud by a panicked non-speaker through narrowband cellular is not intelligible. That single fact is the only thing keeping a device in the loop at all — and you should be able to say it in one breath, because it is now the load-bearing justification for your entire product.

Clash 2 — Tap grid versus speech. The consensus here is suspect.

Four advisors converged on "strip the AI, ship a tap grid." The dissent reviewer was right to be suspicious: four instances running the same cheap move — subtract the AI, find the residue, declare the residue the product — is weak evidence, not strong. The steelman nobody wrote: a panicking layperson does not know which of 28 categories "she's gone grey and floppy" belongs to. Mapping free description onto ontology IDs is precisely the classifier's job, and a grid silently hands that mapping back to the user while claiming to "hand them the structure." Twenty-eight alternatives under stress is search, not structure. The human-factors reviewer added the other half: a wrong tap speaks confidently wrong Japanese in an authoritative synthetic voice, and neither the caller nor the dispatcher can catch it.

Chairman's resolution

The panic-critical payload is one button, not 28. Your own README says the ambulance is dispatched on type + location at ~15 seconds. Every symptom tap happens after dispatch, while the ambulance is already moving and a dispatcher is guiding. So "28 icons under maximum stress" is a scenario your own architecture does not create. Ship the grid — but the drill must measure time-to-first-button and icon-find latency separately, or you are shipping your core assumption untested, which is the exact sin you convict the laptop of.

Clash 3 — Does gutting the runtime AI destroy the Intel story?

The Expansionist's rescue was "the AI moves to registration." The strategy reviewer called that a rationalisation and is right: registration-time work is TTS of an address, not Qwen classification, and your 6.97× / 10.94× were measured on the inference path registration never touches. Do not use that framing — it will not survive a judge who reads your own benchmark files.

03Blind Spots the Council Caught

A · Your existing "PASSED" is void

Phone-to-phone VoLTE negotiates AMR-WB (~50–7000 Hz). A real 119 call routinely lands on AMR-NB (300–3400 Hz) with a voice activity detector. Loudspeaker-to-mic TTS under AGC clamping is exactly the signal a VAD misclassifies as noise and replaces with comfort noise — a failure mode entirely independent of echo cancellation, which nobody named. This invalidates item 13 in OPEN_DECISIONS.md: that test was a WhatsApp call — wideband Opus, no cellular VAD. It does not prove what the register says it proves.

B · The duplex problem

Nobody in the entire council designed for the dispatcher talking back. A recording cannot yield. A dispatcher who cannot interrupt will talk over it, and then neither party hears the other. Worse: while Japanese plays out the loudspeaker, the caller cannot hear the dispatcher's question — playback occupies their only audio channel. There is no STOP button anywhere in any proposal.

C · The downlink is your next product

Every advisor treated this as getting information to the dispatcher. But a 119 dispatcher immediately talks back, in scripted Japanese the caller cannot understand — inside the same 60 seconds. That failure needs no uplink injection, breaks no physics, and works on one device. The Outsider named the same thing from the other end: the frightening part is the nine minutes of 口頭指導 you dismiss in one line. This is where runtime AI genuinely earns its place, and naming it is what makes the ablation an advance rather than a retreat.

D · Liability — raised by no advisor, demolished by one reviewer

"Register 200 households in a weekend" is a 16-year-old, unsupervised, collecting 要配慮個人情報 — conditions, medications, ages of elderly dependents — from strangers in a school hall. No consent form, no APPI data controller, no stated storage location, and a minor cannot sign a data agreement. Beyond that: no incident record, no way to ever learn it was used, no recall path when a household moves and the stored address becomes wrong, and no instruction telling the caller what to do when it fails.

E · The real incumbent is missing from your competitive analysis. Not NET119, not 三者間同時通訳. It is: dial 119, shout in English, then phone the one Japanese-speaking person you know and hand over. You compare yourself only to government services, never to what people actually do.

F · Corrections to your own documents. 49 WAVs, not 46. src/ is 2,089 lines. And the README's 13.3%-cannot-read-Japanese statistic is doing work the product does not do — your interface is in English, including both confirmation screens, so your actual user is an English reader. Say that plainly.

04The Recommendation

Split the product along the only three moments that actually exist, and be honest that the AI currently sits in the wrong one.

1 · Before — registration

No panic, no clock, no second device. The laptop app survives here unchanged, building a household's verified Japanese audio bundle. This is the honest home of your Streamlit/OpenVINO stack. It is not where the 6.97× applies, and you must not claim it is.

2 · During, uplink — the phone in the caller's hand

An offline PWA, ~4 MB, one home-screen icon. One red button that plays 救急です + address + room. Three forced-choice vital buttons. A two-level symptom grid after dispatch. A permanent STOP. Zero AI at runtime — and you say that out loud, as a finding, not a confession.

3 · During, downlink — the unbuilt half

The dispatcher's Japanese, transcribed on-device and matched against a closed set of expected dispatcher questions, shown to the caller in their language. No uplink injection. No physics wall. One device. This is where Whisper and the classifier genuinely belong, and it is the strongest honest thing you can put in front of a judge.

The festival framing — better than what you have now

We ablated our own model. We measured that the uplink carried five bits and did not need a 3B model to carry them — so we took it out and shipped the part that works on the phone people already hold. Then we measured where the model is necessary: understanding the dispatcher. Here is the benchmark for both.

A rigorously documented negative result is judgeable work. Concealing it is the only thing that isn't. This also satisfies your own rule — the pitch follows the truth, rather than the truth being shaped for the pitch.

The single-device verdict, bluntly

Injecting audio into a live call's uplink is impossible on every platform. The self-echo loop — the phone playing its own audio out its own loudspeaker into its own microphone on speakerphone — is unproven, and your existing "PASSED" does not prove it, because it ran over WhatsApp. It may work. If it does, everything above ships. If it does not, the honest product is registration + downlink comprehension — and you must write down what each outcome means before you run the test, so the result cannot be negotiated afterwards.

06The Full Record

Contrarian "It is not untested — it is adversely designed against you"

The single-device path is not "untested," it is adversely designed against you. On a speakerphone call, hardware AEC's reference signal is the speaker feed, not the downlink stream. Your app's media audio goes into that same mixer. You would be asking the phone's DSP to cancel everything coming out of its loudspeaker except the one thing you want it to pass. Worse, the mic is centimetres from a speaker at call volume: AGC clamps, and the caller's own voice is destroyed for the duration. On iOS, media playback during a cellular call is ducked or denied outright. Even in the lucky case where one handset leaks enough signal through, you have a life-safety product whose delivery mechanism is per-handset, per-OS, per-carrier lottery. "It worked on my Pixel" is not a mechanism. Test it — but plan for it failing, because it probably will, and an unreliable pass is worse than a clean fail.

Here is the flaw you're avoiding. Strip out live injection and look at what's left. Your own log concedes a government text+GPS app beats you architecturally. Your README says the only real gap versus 三者間同時通訳 is the room number and the medical history. That gap is a profile.json lookup and a pre-rendered string. It needs no Whisper, no Qwen, no ontology, no OpenVINO, no 511s→73s. The part of your product that is uniquely valuable requires zero AI. The part that requires AI — live symptom classification into 31 terms — is precisely the part a human interpreter already does better, live, on 93.5% of departments, for free.

So yes: a laminated card with your address, 101号室, age, 持病, in Japanese with romaji the caller can read aloud, delivers most of your defensible value at 0% of the technology. Say that out loud before a judge says it for you.

Honest fallback: the app becomes a generator of that pre-call artifact — one phone, no call-time audio path, nothing to inject, no AEC. The AI earns its place only if it does something the card cannot: 口頭指導 in the caller's language, spoken to the caller's ear, after dispatch. That's a delivery-to-caller problem, which has no technical wall.

First Principles "You run a 3B model to move five bits"

Strip the AI out and count what's left: 46 WAV files, 3.9 MB. That is the entire Japanese surface area of this product — every sentence it can ever utter, pre-rendered by tools/build_audio.py, human-verified, closed-set. The classifier does not produce Japanese. It picks which file to play. The irreducible unit of value is a verified Japanese audio sentence.

Now count the bits. At the moment of the call: address, room, age, sex, conditions — all already in profile.json. Zero entropy. Consciousness, breathing, circulation — forced buttons, 3 bits, the model is explicitly banned from touching them. What remains is one selection from symptoms (20) plus events (8): roughly 4–5 bits of genuinely new information.

The product runs Whisper, then a 3-billion-parameter model over an 884-token prompt, then two confirmation loops, to move five bits. OpenVINO's 6.97× and the x64-exclusive 10.94× are real engineering — applied to hauling five bits across a laptop.

So the council is being asked the wrong question. "One device or two?" is downstream. The real question is: why is there a speech input at all? Your own README answers it against you: "a panicking person cannot produce structured, dispatcher-usable information at all — in any language." Correct. The response to cannot produce structure is to hand them the structure, not to infer it from unstructured speech. Speech re-injects exactly the entropy the stored profile was built to eliminate — and every open risk you logged lives in that lane: C1 negation inversion, C2's seven unrun languages, Whisper's "You're free to go." ×70, the English-only confirmation screens.

And a laminated fridge card does not substitute, because a caller who cannot read Japanese cannot pronounce 〇〇区〇〇. The real product is an offline Japanese soundboard with a stored profile. Say that out loud. Then decide, honestly, whether the Intel story survives it — not the reverse.

Expansionist "The 3.9 GB is not the product. The 4 MB is."

Everyone is reasoning as if the asset pile is a model stack. It isn't. data/audio/ is pre-rendered WAVs and data/ontology.json is 31 human-verified terms plus 27 conditions and 13 condition groups. Neither needs Whisper, Qwen, OpenVINO, or a warm anything. The README already concedes the app "never synthesises speech at runtime." That means the inference can happen once, at registration, on a machine that is not the user's, and the household walks away with a phone-sized bundle of verified Japanese audio keyed to their address, their room number, their grandmother's anticoagulants. The hard constraint kills the runtime. It does not touch the output.

Concretely, Nishikasai: the Indian International School in Japan sits in Nishikasai with roughly a thousand families, almost all within walking distance, and its parents are precisely the README's user — foreign resident, fixed registered address, elderly relative on a dependent visa, poor Japanese. One PTA-hall session is not "community partnership," it is a folding table, a laptop, a Hindi-speaking high schooler, and a queue. Add the Edogawa Diwali / Namaste India crowd at Kasai Rinkai and the Nishikasai grocers as QR-sticker surfaces.

And the expert gap is a web form, not a wall. 葛西消防署 runs free public 普通救命講習 on open enrollment — three hours in a room with a serving firefighter instructor. C8 says no paramedic has ever seen this. Booking that course changes C8 this month, without asking permission from anyone.

Undervalued most: the ontology itself. No public dataset maps panicked bystander speech to dispatcher-register Japanese. Publish it CC-BY — it outlives the app.

Outsider "Nobody stands next to the laptop when the emergency starts"

What I picture, and where it breaks. Someone collapses. A person dials 119, puts the phone on the floor on speakerphone, and then — walks to another room, to a laptop, and starts tapping through screens while a dispatcher is talking. That is the break. What a real person does at 3am. They dial 119 and shout in English. Then they call the one Japanese-speaking person they know — a neighbour, a colleague, a spouse's friend — and hand the phone over. That's the actual incumbent, and the README never mentions it. It compares itself to government services, not to the thing people really do.

What I don't believe. The room-number claim is the load-bearing justification for the whole thing, and it's a three-digit number. A terrified person who can't pronounce 呂律が回らない can absolutely say "four zero five."

The document contradicts itself. It says the ambulance is dispatched at 15 seconds and everything after happens while it's en route — then says "inference latency is a polish problem, not a safety one." Fine. But then the 6.97× Intel speedup is a polish story, and it gets four times more page space than what the user actually touches. As a stranger, the ratio of Intel prose to human prose tells me what this was built for.

English interface. The pitch is "for people who don't speak Japanese," but the screens are English. So the user is an English speaker. Say that plainly.

The problem I'd name. Not the first 60 seconds. The nine minutes after, when a dispatcher is trying to talk you through CPR in Japanese and you understand none of it. The README lists that as out of scope in one line. That's the frightening part, and it's the part left alone.

Executor "Run the echo test tonight. It gates everything."

Protocol, exactly: Phone A calls Phone B. B goes in another room with someone holding it. A on speakerphone, media volume max. Play a fixed line out of A's own loudspeaker while the call is live. Listener on B reports: clear / faint-and-warbling / silent. Repeat three times — A flat on a table, A held up, and with someone talking near A. Then repeat the whole thing on the other OS. AEC is implemented differently by Apple and by each Android OEM; one phone proves nothing.

Reading the result: clear → single device works, the laptop dies, the form factor is a PWA. Faint/chopped/pumping → AEC is gating it; retest with wired earbuds in A (the AEC reference moves off the loudspeaker) — if that recovers it, the product ships with "plug in earbuds," a far smaller ask than "own a warm laptop." Silent → the single-device acoustic loop is dead, and you stop designing around it today.

Re-platforming, priced against the actual code: a full port is impossible this week and you should stop considering it. app.py is 1,161 lines of Streamlit session_state spaghetti. Android means no OpenVINO — prefix caching is x64-only, so the 10.94× vanishes — and Whisper + Qwen2.5-3B on a phone is a llama.cpp/ONNX port measured in weeks, not days.

But you don't need the models to ship the phone version. 救急です + your address + 28 symptom/event terms are already rendered. A static PWA with tap-buttons and <audio> playback is two evenings of HTML/JS and delivers the dispatch-critical payload with zero AI on the phone already in the caller's hand.

Validation you can get by Friday: book a 救命講習 at our ward or Edogawa fire station — public, free, walk-in-bookable, and a serving firefighter is in the room for three hours. Show it in the break.
